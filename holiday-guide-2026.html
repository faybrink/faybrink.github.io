A note from Fay

Welcome

Hi neighbor,

Every year I promise myself I'll plan the holidays early, and every year it's suddenly Thanksgiving week and I'm scrolling for parade times in a parking lot. So this year I did the digging for both of us.

Inside you'll find the big nights out, free Santa visits close to home, markets worth a Saturday, where to cut a tree, and a few ways to give back right here in Cy-Fair. I checked every date against what organizers have posted for 2026. Anything that isn't announced yet is marked TBA, so nobody drives to an empty lot.

I also tucked in my gumbo, my mom's cornbread dressing, and a page of house tips I learned the hard way during the 2021 freeze.

Keep it on the fridge, send it to a friend, and if real estate comes up over dessert, you know where to find me.

Merry everything,
Fay

3 quick tree-care tips

Fresh cut first

Ask the farm or lot to take a thin slice off the trunk right before you leave, and get it in water within a few hours.

Water every day

A fresh tree drinks a lot the first week. Keep the water line above the base of the trunk; if it dries out, the cut seals over.

Mind the heat

Keep the tree at least 3 feet from fireplaces, space heaters, candles and heat vents, and turn the lights off when you leave or go to bed.

Chicken & Andouille Gumbo

I use Savoie's jarred roux and I'm not one bit sorry. It saves me an hour at the stove. A deboned rotisserie chicken is my other shortcut. Okra goes in, Tony Chachere's goes on just about everything, and nobody's ever complained.

Ingredients Serves 8

  • 1 lb andouille sausage, sliced into coins
  • 1 onion, 1 green bell pepper and 3 celery ribs, chopped
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • About 3/4 cup Savoie's jarred roux (add more for a thicker, darker pot)
  • 8–10 cups chicken stock
  • 1 rotisserie chicken, skin off, deboned and shredded
  • 2 cups sliced okra
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Tony Chachere's Creole seasoning, to taste
  • Sliced green onions and cooked white rice, for serving

Method

  1. Brown the andouille in a heavy pot. Lift it out and leave the drippings.
  2. Cook the onion, bell pepper and celery in the drippings until soft, about 8 minutes. Add the garlic for 1 minute.
  3. In a separate pot, bring the stock to a simmer and whisk in the roux until it dissolves completely.
  4. Pour the stock into the vegetables, add the sausage and bay leaves, and simmer uncovered 45 minutes.
  5. Stir in the chicken and okra and simmer 30 minutes more. Season with Tony's, pull the bay leaves, and serve over rice with green onions.

Gizzards and all, every Thanksgiving

My Mom's Cornbread Dressing

This is my mom's recipe, and it's on my table every Thanksgiving. Yes, it has chicken gizzards. They make it. Bake the cornbread the day before so it has time to dry out.

Ingredients Serves 10–12

  • 1 lb chicken gizzards
  • 1 pan of day-old cornbread (9x13), crumbled
  • 4 tbsp butter
  • 1 onion, 1 green bell pepper and 3 celery ribs, chopped
  • 3–4 cups broth (use the gizzard cooking liquid)
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • Tony Chachere's Creole seasoning and black pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Simmer the gizzards in salted water about 1 hour, until tender. Save the liquid, then chop the gizzards fine.
  2. Melt the butter and cook the onion, bell pepper and celery until soft.
  3. In a big bowl, combine the cornbread, vegetables and gizzards. Add broth until it's moist but not soupy, then stir in the eggs and season.
  4. Spread in a greased 9x13 dish and bake at 350°F for 45 minutes to 1 hour, until set and golden on top.

Protect the house while you enjoy it

Holiday Home Tips

Hang lights safely

Use lights rated for outdoor use, plug them into GFCI outlets, and have someone hold the ladder. Toss strands with cracked sockets or frayed wire.

Tree & candle safety

Water the tree daily and keep it 3 feet from heat. Never leave a candle burning in an empty room; battery candles look just as good in a window.

Protect your pipes

When a hard freeze is forecast, drip faucets on outside walls, open sink cabinets, cover outdoor spigots, and know where your main water shutoff is before you need it.

HVAC check

Swap the air filter and test the heat before the first cold front, not during it. A tune-up costs far less than an emergency call on a holiday weekend.

Smoke & CO detectors

Test every alarm, replace batteries, and make sure there's a carbon monoxide detector near the bedrooms if you have gas appliances or an attached garage.

Clean the gutters

Clear leaves before winter rains so water drains away from the foundation instead of over the edge.

Stop porch pirates

Ship to a store or parcel locker, require a signature on pricey items, or ask a neighbor to grab boxes while you're away.

Travel prep

Put a few lamps on timers, hold your mail with USPS, set the thermostat to a safe temperature, and give a trusted neighbor your number.
